Catches the subject that is a label rather than a sentence: a bare ticket identifier, one with a fragment hung off it, a noun phrase naming the area worked on, a placeholder standing in for a subject nobody wrote. It also catches the line in the wrong mood, where the opening word is a past form reporting what somebody did, or an -ing form heading a noun phrase that names an activity. It judges the sentence and not the shape of the line around it, so a conventional-commit prefix and a ticket reference riding beside a statement both pass; the prefix and the identifier format are regex-grade and belong in a hook. Commit subjects and pull request titles are judged alike. It ships under the `standalone` tag: the -ing subject is the one case the wording cannot reach, between 0.65 and 0.82 over three attempts and as many runs, because what settled it at 0.90 was naming the example's own opening word in the criteria, which is fitting the rule to its corpus rather than writing it. Several of its criteria still rest on a single example apiece, the short subject and the prefixed one among them, so the wording runs further ahead of the evidence than a preset rule's should; it waits for a wider corpus.
