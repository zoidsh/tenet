Catches the sentence where a quality does the acting: simplicity drives the design, the architecture cares about correctness, the roadmap wants plugins. The regex-grade neighbour is the nominalisation check, `grep -nE '^[A-Z][a-z]+(tion|ment|ity|ance|ness) '` or Vale's `write-good.Nominalizations`, which finds abstract nouns in the subject position and stops there; a rising latency and a growing cost are abstract subjects doing nothing they cannot do, and separating those from an ideal that decides things is the judgement this rule makes.

The line a finding lands on names the plainest sentence without a human subject, not the first one and not necessarily the one that made the window fail. The tenet asks for a human subject in every sentence, so in reference prose a queue or a job doing exactly what it does answers to it as squarely as the abstraction that started the paragraph, and the model picks whichever is barest. The corpus labels each violation's `lines` over the offending run for that reason, and a span may take in a sentence that does have a human subject, because the unit the rule judges is the paragraph and the unit a finding is reported in is the line.

That also means one sentence can sit inside a violation's span here and stand in an ok example elsewhere in this corpus without either label being wrong. The rule judges the paragraph it appears in, so a plain sentence about what a job does is innocent among other plain sentences and is part of the offence in a paragraph an abstraction has been made to act in.
